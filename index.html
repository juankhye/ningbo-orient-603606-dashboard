Executive Summary

The thesis in five sentences

If you skim nothing else, read this. Each bullet is a discrete reason this stock works — pull on any one and the rest still stand.

1 · It's a structural duopoly with a moat dug by physics

Submarine cables fail catastrophically when wrong — 83% of offshore wind insurance claims trace back to them. Developers therefore stick to a handful of qualified suppliers with proven track records, deep-water port access, and proprietary cable-laying vessels. China's market is locked up by three players (Zhongtian 37%, Ningbo Orient 33%, Hengtong 17%) and only six suppliers are National-Grid-approved for HVDC framework contracts. New entrants need years to get qualified, plus port permits that take additional years.

2 · Order book provides multi-year revenue visibility

As of April 2026, the order backlog stands at RMB 18.4bn — roughly 1.7x trailing 12-month revenue. The book is concentrated in high-margin product: RMB 10.4bn in submarine + HV cables (50–60% of which is premium 500kV product), RMB 3.7bn in offshore engineering EPC, and RMB 4.4bn in land cable. Deliveries are scheduled across 2026 and 2027.

3 · 15th Five-Year Plan supercharges China offshore wind

China targets 100GW cumulative offshore wind by 2030, implying ~10GW/year of new installations vs. 5–7GW in 2022–25 — a +39% jump in the run-rate. Submarine cables are 8–13% of total project cost. UBS projects Orient Cable's revenue to grow from RMB 10.8bn (2025) to RMB 28bn (2030) — a 21% CAGR.

4 · Europe is the next leg, validated by Inch Cape delivery

Chinese subsea cables trade ~10% below European peers, with no technical gap — and European suppliers are capacity-constrained. The UK Inch Cape project (RMB 1.8bn) confirmed Orient can meet European technical specs and deliver on time. The UK's AR7 auction allocated 8.4GW, France ran a 10GW tender, and the Hamburg Declaration targets 100GW in the North Sea by 2050.

5 · Margin expansion as the mix turns toward 500kV

Submarine cable GPM was 33.4% in 2025 (UBS estimates it scales to 31% in 2027 and 33% in upside case). Land cable GPM sits at ~8–10%. As more revenue comes from 500kV product — which carries the fattest margins in the entire wind supply chain — overall blended GPM expands from 22.1% (FY25) to 23.7% (2027E) to 26.1% (2030E).

6 · Cheap on its own growth, cheap vs. peers

Trades at 22.6x 2026E P/E on 47% EPS growth, and 18x 2027E P/E on 25% EPS growth — vs. Chinese wind-cable peer average of 23–27x. Stock is below its 27x historical average. UBS PT of RMB 78 implies 23x 2027E P/E (in line with peers) and 28% upside; Morgan Stanley OW at RMB 69.6.

What kills the thesis → A sustained spike in copper prices the company can't pass through to customers; a UK or EU national-security ban on Chinese cable imports (Mingyang precedent); domestic offshore wind project delays from sea-use coordination or military airspace; a successful 500kV+ entry by a new competitor that erodes the oligopoly.
First Principles · 101

What actually is a submarine cable?

A submarine cable is the only physical link between an offshore wind turbine and the grid on land. If it fails, the entire wind farm becomes a stranded asset spinning in the ocean. That brutal asymmetry — cheap component, catastrophic failure cost — is the source of the entire industry's pricing power.

1

Copper conductor

The heart of the cable. Three insulated copper conductors carry three-phase AC power, or one to two for HVDC. Copper is the #1 raw material exposure — 8–13% of total offshore wind project cost is the cable, mostly metal.

2

XLPE insulation

Cross-linked polyethylene, the industry-standard insulator. Only ~10 companies globally can make XLPE that holds at 500kV submarine. Orient produced the world's first 500kV XLPE submarine cable with a soft joint — a key technical milestone.

3

Lead sheath

Watertight metallic barrier. Salt water will destroy any cable it touches — the sheath is what keeps the cable alive for the 25-year design life of the wind farm.

4

Steel armor

Wraps the whole cable in steel wire to survive being dragged across the seabed, anchor strikes, fishing trawlers, and ocean currents. Adds weight — which is why you need specialized cable-laying vessels to install them.

5

Optical fiber

Embedded fiber for SCADA monitoring and grid communication. A "wet mate" connector is required to splice cables in the ocean — Orient developed China's first 3,000m ultra-deepwater umbilical cable connector.

Why this matters → Cables look mundane — they're not. A single 500kV HVDC submarine cable can be 200km long, weigh 100 tonnes per kilometre, and cost €5–10 million per kilometre. A single failure can take a wind farm offline for months because repair requires the same specialized ships used to install it. There aren't many of those ships in the world. That scarcity is the moat.
Voltage Technology Ladder

The kV race: higher voltage = bigger moat

As wind farms move farther offshore and deeper, transmission losses become the binding constraint. Higher voltage = lower losses = longer feasible distance to shore. Each step up the voltage ladder is a multi-year R&D moat. Orient sits at the top of the AC ladder (500kV) and on the second-highest rung of the DC ladder (±525kV), with ±800kV in development.

HVAC vs HVDC, briefly → Alternating current (AC) is the easier, cheaper option but loses ~3–4% of power per 100km of submarine cable, so it becomes uneconomic past ~100–150km. Direct current (HVDC) is harder to manufacture (you need fancy XLPE and converter stations) but loses less than 1% per 1,000km — so it's the only option for deep-sea wind and long interconnectors like the Morocco-UK XLinks project. Orient owns 8.5% of XLCC (a UK HVDC cable producer) and 2.4% of XLinks itself — that's a strategic bet on HVDC adoption in Europe.

Competitive Moat

Five pillars of structural defensibility

The submarine cable business is one of the most concentrated industrial niches in global energy. Orient's quality scorecard rates 13/16 (81%) with infrastructure moat scoring 3.5/4. Each pillar below is independent — together they make the franchise close to unassailable in the near-term.

3.5/4
🔬

UHV/HVDC technology

Fewer than 10 global firms can produce 500kV AC and ±525kV DC submarine cables. The XLPE manufacturing process, soft-joint technology, and high-voltage testing capability take 5–10 years to develop, and even then customers won't qualify you without a track record.

3.5/4
🚢

Proprietary vessel fleet

Two specialised cable-laying ships (Dongfang Haigong 01, 02) and an 18,000-ton transport platform (Dongfang Haigong 07) capable of shipping ultra-long cables directly to Europe. Global shortage of these vessels = sellers' market for combined "manufacture + lay" capability.

3/4
⚓

Port access

Submarine cable spools weigh hundreds of tonnes and must be loaded directly onto installation vessels. Deep-water piers are rare in China — securing permission to build new ones takes years. Existing capacity sits in Ningbo, Yangjiang, Yantai, Beihai. New entrants are physically locked out.

3/4
🏦

Bankability & credit

Because 83% of offshore wind insurance claims trace to cables, developers demand bank and insurance credit endorsements from suppliers. The National Grid maintains a closed roster of just 6 approved HVDC framework suppliers. Insurers won't underwrite projects using untested vendors.

3/4
🔧

Integrated EPC

Developers no longer buy a "cable" — they buy a turnkey delivery package: route planning, manufacturing, installation, testing, lifecycle repair. Orient's integrated capability shifts pricing power upstream and creates switching costs once a project is under way.

International Expansion

The European pipeline

Overseas revenue is targeted to grow from 11.6% of total in 2025 to 15–20% by 2026–30 — UBS calls this the biggest single delta in the bull case. The wedge: Chinese cables price ~10% below European peers with no technical gap, and Europe has run out of domestic capacity.

✓ Inch Cape (UK)

RMB 1.8bn order from SSEN. RMB 910mn delivered in 2025. Grid connection scheduled 2027. First major European delivery — validates technical capability.

XLCC (Scotland, 8.5% stake)

UK-based high-voltage DC cable producer. Equity stake gives Orient an entry into the Scottish HVDC market and a foothold against European incumbents.

XLinks (Morocco-UK, 2.4%)

Massive 4,000km HVDC interconnector to ship Moroccan solar to the UK. Orient's equity stake positions it as preferred cable supplier if the project clears final approvals.

UK AR7 Auction

Allocated 8.4GW of offshore wind capacity. Bid caps raised 11% to GBP81/MWh. Contract terms extended to 20 years (from 15) — restores project IRRs.

France 10GW Tender

Record-size offshore wind tender using 20-year CfD subsidy. Provides stable cash flow guarantees that underpin long-term cable supply contracts.

Hamburg Declaration

EU joint commitment: 100GW installed offshore wind capacity in the North Sea by 2050. Multi-decade demand visibility for HVDC interconnector cable.

Why Chinese cable wins in Europe → European suppliers (Prysmian, Nexans, NKT, Hellenic) are sold out through 2027–28. Their order books are full. New capacity takes 3–5 years to add. Meanwhile EU governments are aggressively expanding offshore wind tenders. Chinese suppliers have spare capacity, are 10% cheaper, and have now demonstrated they can deliver to spec (Inch Cape). The question isn't whether Chinese cable will win share, but how much before geopolitical guardrails (Mingyang precedent) kick in.

Bull vs Bear

The debate, distilled

Click between bull and bear views. Both arguments are real. The bull view is structural; the bear view is cyclical / timing-related.

RMB 19.3 bn backlog visibility

Provides multi-quarter revenue visibility. 50–60% of subsea orders are 500kV product — the highest-margin segment. Backlog secures 2026/2027 deliveries even before new wins.

Technical moat in HVDC structural

Only ~10 global firms with 500kV+ submarine capability. 83% of offshore wind losses are cable-related, creating strict bank/insurance gatekeeping. Six approved State-Grid HVDC suppliers — Orient is one.

European wedge gaining traction growth

29 European projects verified. 10% cost advantage vs European competitors with no quality gap. European suppliers are capacity-constrained. AR7 8.4GW, France 10GW tender, Hamburg Declaration support multi-year demand.

15th Five-Year Plan: 10.6GW/yr policy

+39% vs 14th Plan. 100GW cumulative by 2030 implies 10GW/yr annual installs. UBS estimates 50GW project pipeline supports 10–15GW/yr installations.

Operating leverage margin

FY25 net profit +26% YoY vs revenue +19% YoY. Q1 2026 net profit +32% YoY on rising 500kV mix and tight cost controls. Mix shift to higher-margin product is the engine.

Fortress balance sheet defensive

Net cash RMB 2.16bn. Debt/equity just 9.8%. Generates RMB 1.96bn OCF in 2025. Funds RMB-billion capacity expansions entirely from internal cash flow without external dilution.

Copper input volatility margin risk

Submarine cables are 8–13% of total offshore wind project cost. Copper dominates bill-of-materials. If grid-parity tariff cuts force downstream operators to push costs upstream, Orient's margins compress.

Q2 2025 saw a 49.6% profit drop execution

Delayed deliveries compressed GPM 6.3ppts in Q2 2025. This kind of quarterly variability is structural — submarine cable revenue recognition is lumpy and depends on offshore construction windows.

Capacity expansion = price competition competitive

Zhongtian and Hengtong are also expanding capacity. In a post-subsidy grid-parity world, simultaneous capacity additions could trigger industry-wide ASP cuts and erode the 33% GPM.

Geopolitical risk in Europe tail risk

UK blocked Mingyang from building a wind turbine factory in Scotland on national-security grounds. Chinese submarine cable suppliers could face similar bans — particularly in HVDC interconnectors with critical-infrastructure status.

Installation bottlenecks cap demand demand

The practical annual offshore wind installation ceiling in China and Europe may be below static market expectations. Bottlenecks in sea-use coordination, military airspace, port logistics, grid connections, and installation vessel availability limit what can actually be built.

Technical downgrade signal sentiment

William O'Neil removed Buy in November 2025 after the stock broke 50-day moving average. Market temporarily penalising on near-term sentiment despite intact fundamentals. Huatai trimmed 2026 net profit forecast 8.7% on project-timing concerns.

6 · Geopolitical / local-content haircut

European policy is moving against Chinese energy-infrastructure suppliers. Not yet outright bans on subsea cable — but the wall is being built brick by brick.

France AO10 — 60% local content wall

10 GW tender explicitly mandates ≥60% European supply chain for submarine cables under NZIA standards. Effectively excludes Chinese cable from this entire tranche. Equivalent of ~3,000 km of cable demand off-limits.

EU FDI review · €100M threshold

Investments >€100 M in net-zero manufacturing trigger 60–105 day FDI review. Forces mandatory JVs with EU partners + IP sharing + localized R&D. Makes greenfield European capacity build economically unviable for Orient.

Mingyang Scotland · April 2024 EU anti-subsidy probe

UK blocked Mingyang's Scotland turbine factory on national security. EU launched anti-subsidy probe on Chinese wind turbines April 2024. Subsea cable arguably more critical infrastructure — precedent extension is the real tail risk.

9 · What flips us short — quantified tripwires

Six specific monitorable thresholds. Each one is observable in quarterly disclosure or public tender data. If two or more trip, the thesis is broken — not "should be reviewed."

TRIPWIRE 01 · DEMAND
China offshore wind bidding falls below 2 GW in any quarter
Domestic demand engine relies on ~10 GW/year installations. A sub-2 GW quarter implies installation rate is closer to 14FYP pace (5–7 GW/yr) than 15FYP target. Bidding data is public; quarterly review.
Threshold: Q1 or Q2 2026 bidding < 2.0 GW
TRIPWIRE 02 · MARGIN
500 kV submarine ASP falls below RMB 12 M/km in a major tender
Management-guided ASP is RMB 14 M/km. A 14% ASP haircut at the highest-margin product = post-grid-parity pricing collapse, mirroring 2021 40% turbine price decline. Submarine GPM compresses from 35% to ~25%.
Threshold: any 500 kV award < RMB 12 M/km
TRIPWIRE 03 · INVENTORY
Submarine cable inventory grows >25% QoQ without matching revenue ramp
819 km of inventory at end-2025 should convert to revenue in 2026. If inventory keeps growing AND revenue is flat, downstream installation is stalling. Replays Q2 2025 pattern (−49.6% net profit YoY).
Threshold: Q2 26 inventory > 1,025 km · revenue ≤ Q1
TRIPWIRE 04 · COMPETITIVE
Top-3 China oligopoly share falls below 80%
Currently 87% (ZTT 37%, Orient 33%, Hengtong 17%). If a new entrant successfully qualifies for State Grid framework on 500 kV+ and starts winning provincial tenders, the oligopoly is destabilising. Watch bidding records.
Threshold: top-3 share < 80% over rolling 4-quarter window
TRIPWIRE 05 · GEOPOLITICAL
Named UK/EU national-security review action targets Chinese submarine cable
Mingyang precedent + April 2024 anti-subsidy probe + NZIA local content already point this way. A specific binding restriction on subsea cable (vs turbines) would compress overseas revenue target from 20% to single digits.

TRIPWIRE 06 · COPPER
Sustained copper spike unmatched by ASP pass-through
Copper = 37% of cable BOM. Orient hedges 100% of subsea orders via futures, but new orders need ASP renegotiation. If copper sustains +30% and Orient cannot pass it through, submarine GPM compresses 6-8 ppt. Already happened (early 2022 Credit Suisse note flagged this).
Threshold: subsea GPM < 28% for two consecutive quarters

Long-Term Option Value

The floating offshore wind option

A free option, essentially. If floating wind reaches grid parity in the 2030s, Orient is one of three or four global companies that can build the dynamic cables that connect floating turbines to the seabed. That cable is harder than fixed-bottom — it moves with the turbine in waves and currents, requiring custom fatigue-resistant engineering.

Why floating wind matters

Most of the world's best wind resources are in water too deep for fixed-bottom turbines (>60m). Floating platforms unlock that — but they require dynamic cables that can survive 25 years of constant motion. William O'Neil projects 80% cost reduction by 2050, enabling grid-parity commercial development.

Markets opened by floating: deep Atlantic (UK, France, Norway, US Pacific), Japan, Korea, Taiwan, parts of the Mediterranean. All currently uneconomic, all become massive demand if floating reaches grid parity.
